Synthetic UX research interview created for Phase 3 dataset development.

Moderator: We'll evaluate the wishlist and recommendation experience. Please browse a few products and save items you might buy later.
Participant: I usually browse for inspiration, so recommendations are important to me.
Moderator: Start browsing.
Participant: The recommendations are relevant at first. I'm seeing products similar to things I would wear.
Moderator: Save one item.
Participant: The heart button is familiar. I know what it means without reading anything.
Moderator: Save another item.
Participant: That worked too. I can see a small animation, so I know the action happened.
Moderator: Now open your saved items.
Participant: I see a wishlist icon at the top, so this is much easier than I expected.
Moderator: Compare the items.
Participant: I can see the products, prices, and sizes, but I can't compare them side by side.
Moderator: What would you like to compare?
Participant: Mainly price, material, and rating. Right now I have to open each product separately.
Moderator: Open the first item.
Participant: The product page loads quickly.
Moderator: Open the second item.
Participant: This one takes noticeably longer. I'm not sure if the website is slower or if the page has more content.
Moderator: How does that affect you?
Participant: It isn't a major problem, but it interrupts the browsing flow.
Moderator: Can you check whether either item is available in your size from the wishlist?
Participant: Yes, but I have to open each product. I wish the wishlist showed availability directly.
Moderator: Would that change how likely you are to use the wishlist?
Participant: Definitely. I would use it more if I could manage items without reopening every product.
Moderator: Now imagine you want to buy an item that has gone out of stock.
Participant: I would expect the wishlist to warn me. Instead, I have to open it to discover that it's unavailable.
Moderator: What would you recommend?
Participant: Show stock status on the wishlist and let me remove or compare items there.
Moderator: Overall, what worked well?
Participant: Saving items is excellent. The heart icon and animation make the action obvious.
Moderator: What is the biggest weakness?
Participant: The wishlist feels like a storage place rather than a decision-making tool. It should help me compare and decide what to buy.
Moderator: Would you recommend the website?
Participant: Yes. The recommendations are good and the basic browsing is enjoyable. The wishlist just needs more useful information.
